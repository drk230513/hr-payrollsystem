Terms of service

Last updated 22 August 2026

This is a draft and is not yet in force. It sets out the shape of the agreement and the points that need deciding. It has not been reviewed by a solicitor and should not be relied on by us or by you. Nothing on this page forms a contract.

Passages marked [like this] are decisions still to be made.

1. Who this is between

These terms will govern use of the HR & Payroll System supplied by Open Source AI Ltd (company number 16933778) to a customer organisation. They apply alongside the data processing agreement, which governs personal data.

2. What we provide

Access to the software as a hosted service, for the customer's own payroll and HR administration, for the number of employees stated in the order.

What the service is not: we supply software, not advice. We are not accountants, tax advisers or employment lawyers. Deciding what someone should be paid, how they are classified for tax, and whether a deduction is lawful remains the customer's responsibility.

3. Statutory submissions

Until HMRC software recognition and a BACS Service User Number are in place, the service prepares submissions and payment files but does not send them. The customer remains responsible for filing with HMRC and instructing payment. This section will be revised when accreditation completes.

4. What the customer is responsible for

  • The accuracy of the data entered, including salaries, tax codes and bank details
  • Reviewing each payroll run and approving it before payment
  • Keeping account credentials secure, and using multi-factor authentication where required
  • Meeting their own obligations as an employer and as a data controller

The service raises exceptions for review. It does not remove the requirement for a person to check the payroll before money moves, and is not designed to.

This section needs a solicitor, not a template. A payroll error can cause an underpayment to HMRC, penalties, and employee claims. The cap, the exclusions, and how they interact with professional indemnity cover have to be drafted deliberately.

Whatever is agreed, on termination the customer will be able to export their complete payroll records in a usable format. Payroll records must be retained for six years, so no customer should be left unable to meet that obligation because they changed supplier.
