Data processing agreement

Last updated 22 August 2026

This is a draft for review, not an executable agreement. A data processing agreement is a contract with terms that Article 28 of the UK GDPR makes mandatory. Getting it wrong exposes both parties to enforcement.

Have a solicitor draft or review this before signing anything. Public sector customers will read it properly, and a template found online is a false economy when the subject is other people's payroll records. This page exists so a solicitor starts from a structure rather than a blank page.

1. Roles

The customer is the controller of their employees' personal data. Open Source AI Ltd is the processor. The customer decides what is processed and why; we process it on their instructions.

3. Our obligations

Article 28(3) requires a processor to commit to each of the following. We will:

  1. process personal data only on the customer's documented instructions, including on transfers outside the UK, unless required otherwise by law — in which case we will tell the customer first, unless the law forbids it;
  2. ensure everyone authorised to process the data is under a duty of confidence;
  3. take the security measures required by Article 32 — see security;
  4. engage sub-processors only on the terms in section 4;
  5. help the customer respond to requests from individuals exercising their rights;
  6. help the customer with security, breach notification, impact assessments and consultation with the ICO;
  7. delete or return all personal data at the end of the agreement, as the customer chooses, unless the law requires us to keep it;
  8. make available the information needed to demonstrate compliance, and allow and contribute to audits.

5. International transfers

Customer payroll data is stored and processed in the United Kingdom. We will not transfer it outside the UK without the customer's prior written agreement and an appropriate transfer mechanism.

This is a different position from enquiries sent through our website, which are handled by a US provider — see the privacy notice.

6. Security

The measures we apply are described on the security page and form part of this agreement. In summary: a separate database per customer, encryption of bank details at column level, separated database roles so the application cannot read bank details, append-only audit, and separate per-customer backups.

7. Personal data breaches

We will notify the customer without undue delay and in any event within [24 / 48] hours of becoming aware of a personal data breach affecting their data, with the information the customer needs to meet their own 72-hour obligation to the ICO.

8. Assisting with individuals' rights

If an individual contacts us directly, we will not respond on the customer's behalf. We will pass it to the customer without undue delay and help them answer it.

9. Audit

We will provide the information reasonably needed to demonstrate compliance and allow audits on reasonable notice, no more than [frequency] a year unless required by a regulator or following a breach.

10. Deletion and return

On termination the customer may export their complete records. We will delete their data within [period] of a written request, subject to any legal retention requirement.

Because each customer has their own database, deletion is a genuine removal rather than a flag on a shared table.
